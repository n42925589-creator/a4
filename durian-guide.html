The durian guide

Where durian comes from, how to choose and open one, how to store it and how to enjoy it with care.

Meet the durian

The king of fruits

Durian grows on tall trees in Southeast Asia, especially in Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia. The fruit is covered in a hard, thorny husk, and inside are soft, creamy pods of flesh around large seeds.

Its name comes from the Malay word duri, meaning thorn. Loved by many and famous for its powerful aroma, it’s often called the king of fruits.

Opening a durian

Four careful steps

  1. Protect

    Wear thick gloves or wrap the fruit in a towel. Work on a stable, covered surface.

  2. Find the seams

    Turn the fruit over and look for faint lines running from the base.

  3. Prise open

    Insert a sturdy knife into a seam at the base and twist gently to split the husk.

  4. Lift the pods

    Pull the sections apart and lift out the pods with your fingers or a spoon.

Buying & storing

Choosing and keeping durian

  • Choose fruit that feels heavy for its size with a fresh, sweet aroma near the stem.
  • Avoid fruit with wet patches, mould or a sour, fermented smell.
  • Pre-packed pods should look plump and dry, not watery.
  • Refrigerate opened pods in an airtight box and eat within two to three days.
  • Freeze extra pods for up to three months.

Do

  • Check local rules before carrying durian on transport or into hotels.
  • Use a sturdy knife and keep fingers clear of the thorns.
  • Share: one fruit usually serves several people.

Don’t

  • Eat the seeds raw.
  • Leave opened durian unwrapped in a shared fridge.
  • Let young children handle the spiky husk.
